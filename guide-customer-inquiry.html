Follow One Customer Inquiry.

A practical way to see where calls, messages, and next steps can fall through.

← Back to resources

Begin Where the Customer Begins

Choose a recent inquiry and follow its actual route. Did it arrive by phone, email, a website form, or a social message? Who saw it first? What happened while the person responsible was serving another customer?

Look across all the places inquiries arrive. A process that works well for calls can still leave email or website messages waiting. The customer experiences your business as one relationship, even when the messages enter through different tools.

Give the Next Step an Owner

Acknowledging a message and completing the customer’s request are different actions. Record who is responsible for each one. If a message needs an estimate, appointment, or technical answer, make the handoff explicit.

Agree what information the next person needs, where the request is recorded, and what happens if the usual owner is unavailable. Avoid relying on a forwarded message as the only evidence that someone has accepted responsibility.

Make Follow-Up Visible

Use a place your team can actually maintain to record the inquiry, its current owner, and the next action. The right starting point depends on the business; a more complicated system is not automatically more useful.

Look for requests that are open without a next action, messages that have changed owners several times, and customers who had to ask twice. Those examples can reveal a missing handoff or an unclear completion point.

Review the Experience, Not Just the Count

A high number of answered messages does not tell you whether customers got a useful response. Read or discuss a small set of real examples with the team. Consider how long customers waited and whether their next step was clear.

Choose one improvement, then review the same part of the journey again. Response expectations should reflect your hours, capacity, customer needs, and the work involved.

A useful place to start

Take one recent inquiry. Can you identify its owner, its next action, and what the customer was told to expect?
