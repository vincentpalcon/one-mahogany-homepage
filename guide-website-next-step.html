Does Your Website Make the Next Step Clear?

Review the customer journey from recognition to a useful conversation.

← Back to resources

Enter as a Customer

Start on the page a customer is likely to see first. Ask whether it helps them recognize their situation and understand why the business is relevant. Read it without relying on knowledge that only your team has.

A list of services can be accurate and still leave a visitor unsure where to begin. Consider the question the person is trying to answer at each point, and whether the page answers it in familiar language.

Give Each Page a Job

Decide what the visitor should understand after reading the page. A home page may create recognition. Another page may explain the process, establish trust, or make contacting the business easier.

Look at the transition between pages. Does the next link follow naturally from what the visitor just learned? Consistent language and a clear route can help the site feel like one connected experience.

Review the Contact Path

Try to contact the business on a phone-sized screen and with a keyboard. Check whether labels are clear, fields are readable, and the form asks only for information needed to begin.

Explain what happens next. Do not ask a visitor to select a technical solution before they have described the problem. Provide another contact route where appropriate and make any limitations of the form clear.

Use Real Questions to Improve It

Ask someone unfamiliar with the business to explain what they think you help with and what they would do next. Notice where they hesitate or need an explanation.

Combine those observations with the customer questions your team already hears. Improve the parts that create confusion, then check the same path again. The aim is a clearer journey tied to the business’s actual customers.

A useful place to start

Choose one customer question for each page to answer. Then test whether its main link creates the next useful question.
