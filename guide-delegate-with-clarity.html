Before You Delegate, Make the Work Clear.

Separate the decisions that need your judgment from the recurring work that needs ownership.

← Back to resources

Notice What Keeps Returning

For a few working days, note the tasks and questions that come back to you. Distinguish decisions that need your experience from repeated work that reaches you because nobody else has clear ownership.

Do not assume that every recurring interruption should be delegated immediately. Some work needs a better process, better information, or a clearer boundary before another person can take responsibility for it.

Define What Complete Looks Like

Choose one recurring task. Explain the outcome, the information needed, the expected standard, and how completion is recorded. Show an ordinary example and a situation that should be escalated.

The goal is enough clarity for someone to act without reconstructing your judgment every time. Document the important decisions in language the person doing the work can use. Leave room for questions about exceptions.

Agree the Decision Boundaries

Discuss what the person can decide, what requires your approval, and which situations need immediate attention. Agree how and when you will review the work. Different tasks need different levels of visibility.

Check capacity as well as skill. Giving ownership to someone who already has a full workload can simply move the bottleneck. The surrounding schedule and information need to support the responsibility.

Review and Refine Together

Begin with a manageable scope. Review actual examples rather than checking every action continuously. Ask what was unclear, which information was missing, and where the work still depended on you.

Use the review to improve the task definition and boundaries. Useful delegation should create dependable ownership while giving the business owner enough visibility to remain accountable.

A useful place to start

Choose one task that repeatedly returns to you. Write its desired outcome, completion standard, decision limits, escalation point, and review rhythm.
